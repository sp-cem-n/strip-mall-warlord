# What the player actually does

**In one line:** you decide what each storefront in a dying strip mall *is*, and the crew those shops send out fights its own way across the parking lot.

## Moment to moment (30 seconds at a time)
Watch the lot. Your crew walks out, trades hits, and either takes the row or limps back. When they lose, the log says why in one sentence ("Nobody on the crew can handle the Loss Prevention Associate. That's a job for a Fixer."). You respond with one of two moves:
- **Renovate** a storefront: more rent, and a tougher crew member.
- **Rebrand** a storefront: swap the shop, and with it the crew member. The building keeps its renovations.

## Session to session (a few minutes, a few times a day)
Open the game → read "While you were away" (rent collected, rows taken) → spend the pile → check what the next lot is weak to (the colored badges) → set the lineup → close the tab. When the crew can't go any further, they take a **smoke break** and the **Pave it** stop on the zone track lights up. Bulldozing resets the plaza for Asphalt, and the next run goes visibly faster.

## Where it's idle
Rent comes in every second, and fights start, resolve and retry on their own, including while the game is closed (up to 8h). A crew that hits a wall doesn't grind out hundreds of hopeless fights. It goes on a smoke break and retries less and less often. **No decision is ever on a timer.** Nothing is lost by being away.

## Where the joy comes from
1. **The lineup click.** You rebrand two storefronts, and the lot that walled you for ten minutes falls in one fight. You read the problem and fixed it.
2. **The plaza filling up.** Vacant "FOR LEASE" windows turn into lit signs, captured storefronts arrive in the rival lot's own brick, and the strip gets longer.
3. **The text.** Dry job titles ("Oversees eleven stores and a golf cart."), the Lawyer's C&D, the one sign letter that's always burnt out.
4. **The reset.** Bulldozing and blasting through lots that took twenty minutes last time.

## Where active play fits (and where it doesn't)
**Walk-ins** happen only while the game is open: about every 90s, someone shows up at a random storefront to pawn something (a Maglite, a Case of Red Bull). Click within 25s and the crew hits 50% harder for a minute. Ignore it and nothing bad happens. It never happens offline and never stacks.

Measured with the balance bot (seed 1991): grabbing every walk-in takes the Outlet Mall at **13:38 vs 16:13** idle, and reaches the Lifestyle boss row by 60 min instead of row 7. That's faster, but it's not required, so the idle base holds.

Ruled out: tapping to attack, timed minigames, anything you lose by not clicking. All of those punish idle players.
